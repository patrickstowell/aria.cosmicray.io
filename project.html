CosmicRay 

What is CosmicRay?

CosmicRay is a project designed to find out more about the activities beneath the Greenland Ice Sheet, using revolutionary technology to measure melting with particles from space.

From supernova to snow, these particles, alternatively known as cosmic rays, are produced by events such as solar flares and planetary explosions, and are propelled through space to earth, where they collide with the atmosphere and separate into a shower of particles which then land all over the world.

CosmicRay aims to create and use sensors on the ice sheet to monitor its melting habits. To do this, the sensors are equipped to detect the amount of these particles, also known as muons and neutrons, that are in the surrounding snow and ice. Since these neutrons get absorbed by hydrogen, when water or snow cover increases around a sensor, it means less neutrons are detected.

Where is CosmicRay based and who is behind it?

CosmicRay is based at the University of Sheffield, being spearheaded by the department of Mathematical and Physical Science.

Dr. Patrick Stowell, researcher and lecturer in particle physics, is the lead scientist behind the project. In collaboration and with funding from ARIA, the Advanced Research and Invention Agency, he has been able to develop pre-existing work in particle detection and apply it to a much needed field. Alongside current postdoc, Rob Foster, the team have been hard at work, building and testing their detectors.

This project is in accordance with the ARIA group. As part of the “Forecasting Tipping Points” programme, CosmicRay aims to shed light on the Greenland Ice Sheet, a shared goal across several programme projects. Tipping points may seem like an abstract idea, but they represent the global systems that are currently at risk, and may reach an inevitable state of loss.
